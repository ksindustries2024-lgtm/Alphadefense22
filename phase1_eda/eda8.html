EDA-1 Integrity & Loading

Merge strategy
train_transaction (left) ⟵ merged ⟵ train_identity (right), joined on TransactionID.
Why this direction
Every row of train_transaction carries the fraud label and must be kept regardless of identity-match status. Identity/device checks were only performed for a subset of transactions — so a row with no match in train_identity gets its identity columns filled with NaN, not dropped. NaN here reflects "no identity check was performed," which becomes directly relevant later (EDA-3's confounder finding).

EDA-2 Target Distribution & Class Imbalance

Fraud rate
3.51% fraud vs. 96.49% legitimate.
Why accuracy is the wrong metric
A model predicting "legitimate" for every row scores 96.49% accuracy while catching zero fraud. Accuracy is meaningless under this imbalance — precision/recall (and later, precision@k) are the real targets.
Fix applied
Class imbalance ratio = legitimate ÷ fraud ≈ 27.5, passed to XGBoost's scale_pos_weight. Effect: misclassifying a fraud case as legitimate is penalized ~27.5× more heavily during training than the reverse error — forcing the model to actively prioritize recall on the minority class rather than defaulting to the majority-class shortcut.

EDA-3 Missing Value Structure

✓ VERIFIED CORRELATION
Confirmed finding
  • ProductCD = W: low null rate in identity fields, low fraud rate (2.0%)
  • ProductCD = C: high null rate in addr2, high fraud rate (11.7%)
Confounder mechanism
Both patterns trace back to a shared root cause — ProductCD category itself — rather than nulls directly causing or predicting fraud. Treating "more nulls" as a universal fraud signal would have been a modeling error: the real driver is which product type the transaction belongs to, and null rate is just a downstream symptom of that.
⚠ UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS — not in the model, not a claimed finding
Why ProductCD categories differ in null rate and fraud rate to begin with — e.g. differing verification requirements, transaction size, or merchant category by product type — was not investigated in this project. This remains an open question, explicitly separated here from the verified correlation above.

What VIF measures
Variance Inflation Factor quantifies how redundant a column is given the other columns — a high VIF means the column is largely reconstructable from a linear combination of others, adding instability/noise to a linear model without adding independent signal.
Pipeline
Block discovery (12 blocks, by identical null-count) → Group-aware imputation → Block-scoped VIF → Within-block clustering (pairwise corr ≥ 0.8, ALL-member rule) → Keep-by-target-correlation
Keep/drop rule
Within each cluster, the column with the highest |corr(isFraud)| survives as the cluster's representative — VIF is what grouped columns into clusters, not what decides the survivor. If even the best member's correlation is ≤ 0.01, the entire cluster is dropped rather than keeping a token low-signal survivor.
Scoping limitation (documented, not fixed)
VIF/clustering are block-scoped only — a genuine cross-block redundancy would not be caught by this method. Full 292×292 VIF was judged intractable/noisy relative to the benefit.

EDA-5 Categorical & Cardinality Encoding

CardinalityTechniqueColumns
≤ 15 unique valuesOne-hot (pd.get_dummies)22 (ProductCD, card4, card6, M1–M9, id_-block, DeviceType)
> 15 unique valuesSmoothed, time-respecting target encoding10 (card1/2/3/5, addr1/2, email domains, id_31, DeviceInfo)
Target encoding formula
(fraud_count + K × 0.0351) / (total_count + K), K = 10
Why 0.0351 and not 0 or 0.5
0.0351 is the dataset's global fraud rate. For a category never seen before (or seen only once), your best unbiased guess is "behaves like the average category" — not 0% (assumes impossible certainty of safety) and not 50% (assumes maximum uncertainty, which overstates risk). Smoothing toward the global rate is the principled default, not an arbitrary choice.
Worked example — why smoothing is necessary
A category seen exactly once, where that one occurrence was fraud, has a raw ratio of 1/1 = 100% — absurd confidence from a sample size of one. With K=10 smoothing: (1 + 10×0.0351) / (1+10) ≈ 0.123 — pulled most of the way back toward the global rate, with only partial weight given to the single observed data point.
Leakage prevention
Computed via groupby(col)['isFraud'].cumsum() - isFraud and groupby(col).cumcount() on TransactionDT-sorted data — each row's own isFraud value is excluded from its own encoding, and only prior (time-respecting) history contributes.

EDA-6 Correlation & Leakage Audit

Operation 2 — leakage check (feature vs. target)
Every feature's individual correlation with isFraud was scanned. Max |corr| = 0.287 (V257, card1_encoded) — well under the 0.5–0.6 threshold that would suggest a leaked proxy for the target.

Conclusion: no single feature leaks fraud information. Fraud is predicted by a pattern across many features, not any one column. card1_encoded's correlation was specifically interrogated and confirmed as real signal (card reuse in fraud is genuine, pre-transaction-available behavior), not a leak.
Operation 3 — redundancy check (feature vs. feature)
Cross-column correlation matrix, threshold |corr| ≥ 0.9. Same-original-column one-hot pairs (e.g. card6_credit/card6_debit) ignored as structurally-forced artifacts. Genuine cross-pipeline duplicates resolved via interpretability tie-break (kept ProductCD_W over V1, ProductCD_S over V41) when |corr(isFraud)| was too close to call, else by higher |corr(isFraud)|.

Why interpretability wins on ties
This is a fraud model that will run through SHAP explainability and likely face compliance/audit scrutiny (SAR justification, adverse-action explanations). When two features tie on predictive power, keeping the interpretable one (ProductCD_W) over the anonymized one (V1) costs nothing in performance but buys a defensible explanation — a SHAP value attached to a named business category means something to an auditor; the same value attached to an anonymized Vesta feature does not.
Operation 4 — post-drop re-verification
Confirmed each target-encoded column was computed only from its own source column + isFraud — never from another feature column — so dropping unrelated redundant columns cannot retroactively invalidate a surviving encoding.

EDA-7 Temporal Structure & Train/Test Split

Why chronological, not random
A random split lets the model train on transactions that occur chronologically after test-set transactions — effectively letting it "see the future" during training. In production, the model will never have access to future data when scoring a live transaction, so a random split would produce inflated, unrealistic validation performance that collapses in deployment.
Split method
Sort by TransactionDT, then 80/20 index-cutoff split via .iloc — no shuffle.

Categorical encoding for modeling
Label Encoder (not "OrdinalEncoder" loosely — the two were compared): converts categorical strings to integers, handles unseen categories at inference by not crashing (assigns a fallback rather than erroring), fit only on training data to avoid test-set leakage into the encoding scheme.
